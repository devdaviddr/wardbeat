Roadmap & next steps

The honest gap list — what today’s slice deliberately leaves out, and what would take WardBeat from a working demo to production-grade inside a real trust.

Product & clinical AI & models Evaluation & quality Operability & platform

Today’s WardBeat makes ward flow visible and barriers actionable, on synthetic data, behind a human gate. That is deliberate: everything shippable is grounded, cited, and recommend-only. This page is the other side of that discipline — the things left out on purpose, grouped by what they unlock. Each item says what it is, why it matters, and what it unlocks, so the roadmap reads as a set of reasoned decisions rather than a wishlist.

Phase 1 · today

Visible & actionable

Phase 2 · next

Admit/discharge so beds turn over, guardrails and editable config, streaming answers — the product and AI items below.

Real data & real operations

01

Product & clinical

The clinical surface WardBeat covers today stops at the edge of action: it reads, structures, and recommends, but a human still admits, discharges, and picks up the phone. Closing that loop — safely — is the product frontier.

Close the bed-lifecycle loop

Next product slice

What. Add admit and discharge so a bed actually turns over inside the system, not just on the board. Today an encounter opens and collects notes, but the closing move — the freed bed — happens outside WardBeat.

  • Why it matters. A freed bed is the whole point. Until the lifecycle closes in-system, WardBeat can show that a patient is fit and cleared but cannot reflect the turnover, so the net bed position stays a partial picture.
  • What it unlocks. Real occupancy in real time, an accurate net-bed number, and the foundation every downstream flow feature needs — you cannot forecast demand honestly without truthful discharges.

Today WardBeat makes the flow visible and the barriers actionable; admit and discharge stay with the clinician and the systems of record.

Write-back to real systems

Behind a human gate

What. The action agent is deliberately recommend-only — approving a suggestion writes an action_audit row and nothing leaves the system. A future integration could dispatch to pharmacy (TTOs), transport, and social care via their systems of record, each behind an explicit human approval.

  • Why it matters. Chasing barriers by hand is exactly the coordination cost WardBeat exists to remove. But an AI that silently sends orders is unsafe; the gate is the safety property, not a limitation to design away.
  • What it unlocks. A worklist that doesn’t just surface the next step but completes it — with a person approving every dispatch and a full audit trail — turning the board from a viewer into a coordination tool.

Today approval is an audited recommendation only; no message reaches pharmacy or transport.

Real data via FHIR / an EHR

Production prerequisite

What. The whole system runs on synthetic data today. A production path needs a read integration with the trust’s EHR over HL7 FHIR, plus an information-governance review — keeping the private, in-tenant posture from the Azure design.

  • Why it matters. Synthetic notes prove the pipeline; they cannot prove clinical value. Real notes are messier, and the numbers only mean something once they run against them — under the right governance.
  • What it unlocks. A live board on a real ward, and the de-identified real notes that harden every eval set and enable fine-tuning downstream. This is the gate between demo and pilot.

Today every note is synthetic, with zero patient information anywhere in the system.

House-wide flow

Scope expansion

What. Extend from a single ward to a whole-site view, with a site/ops persona and cross-ward transfers.

  • Why it matters. Real bed pressure is a site problem — a bed freed on one ward relieves a queue on another. A single-ward view can’t see that trade.
  • What it unlocks. Site-level coordination, transfer decisions with the full picture, and the ops persona that manages flow across wards rather than within one.

Today WardBeat is scoped to one ward at a time.

02

AI & models

The AI plane is intentionally conservative: grounding and allow-lists as the safety net, a single small model per capability, read-only config. Each item below either formalises a safety property that is currently implicit, or extends quality once real labelled data exists.

NeMo Guardrails as an explicit safety rail

What. Grounding plus allow-lists are the current defences. A dedicated NeMo Guardrails layer would formalise input/output policy in one place.

Why. The safety behaviour exists but is spread across prompt design and validation code — a guardrails layer makes it a declared, testable policy.

Unlocks. A single auditable boundary for what the models may accept and emit, easier to review with a clinical-safety lens.

Editable AI configuration

What. GET /config is read-only today. Editing model ids and limits from the UI needs a config store and a security review — the Settings card is the read-only first step.

Why. Operators will want to tune models without a redeploy, but a writable config surface is an attack surface.

Unlocks. Per-deployment tuning through the UI, once the store and review land.

Surface the extraction token budget

What. NIM_EXTRACT_MAX_TOKENS exists in the AI-plane settings but is not yet in GET /config; add it once the field is wired end to end so the Settings card can show it.

Why. The budget shapes extraction cost and truncation behaviour — operators should see it.

Unlocks. A complete, honest Settings view with no hidden knobs.

Streaming answers

What. The copilot answers in one shot; SSE streaming would deliver tokens as they’re generated.

Why. Longer policy answers feel slow when they arrive all at once, even when total latency is fine.

Unlocks. Better perceived latency and a more responsive copilot for open-ended questions.

Per-capability model choice & fine-tuning

What. Allow a different model per task, and fine-tune extraction and recommendation on real labelled data once it’s available.

Why. One model sized for everything is a sensible default, not an optimum; tasks differ.

Unlocks. Higher quality and lower cost per capability, and a path to models tuned on the trust’s own notes.

Active-learning loop & confidence calibration

What. The action_audit trail — what humans approve or dismiss — is a labelled signal. Feed it back to improve extraction and recommendation over time, and calibrate confidence alongside.

Why. Every human decision is free ground truth; discarding it wastes the best signal the system has.

Unlocks. Quality that compounds with use, and confidence scores that mean what they say.

03

Evaluation & quality

AI quality here is already a number, not a feeling: four harnesses exit non-zero below gate. The gap is turning those gates into an automatic, always-on guarantee, and enriching the metrics with methods that catch subtler failures.

Run the eval gates in CI

Automation

What. The four eval harnesses already exit non-zero below their gate. Wire them into CI (once CI is re-introduced) so a regression fails the build, not a demo.

  • Why it matters. A gate only protects you if it runs every time. Run by hand, it catches problems late — or not at all.
  • What it unlocks. A model or prompt change that drops quality is blocked at the PR, not discovered in front of a clinician. This is why re-introducing CI is a prerequisite for several roadmap items.

Today the gates run locally, on demand.

Richer metrics

Quality depth

What. Add RAGAS faithfulness for retrieval, LLM-as-judge rubrics for open-ended answers, and regression tracking of eval scores over time.

  • Why it matters. Hit-rate and groundedness catch the obvious retrieval failures; faithfulness and judged rubrics catch the subtle ones — a plausible answer that quietly drifts from its source.
  • What it unlocks. Confidence in open-ended answers, and a trend line that shows quality moving over releases instead of a single pass/fail per run.

Today metrics are per-run F1, hit-rate, groundedness, rank correlation, and numeric-consistency — with no history kept.

Bigger, real-derived eval sets

Harder numbers

What. Today’s labels are synthetic. Grow the eval sets from de-identified real notes to harden the numbers.

  • Why it matters. Synthetic notes are cleaner than reality; a score against them is an optimistic estimate. Real notes stress the extractor where it actually breaks.
  • What it unlocks. Eval numbers you can trust for a go/no-go on a real ward — depends on the FHIR/EHR read and IG review from Product & clinical.

Today every labelled example is synthetic.

04

Operability & platform

WardBeat runs today from Docker, single-instance, with logs, health/config endpoints, and evals for observability. Production operations need to see cost and latency per call, scale horizontally, and deploy through a repeatable pipeline.

Observability

Tracing, cost & latency metrics

  • What. OpenTelemetry spans across Next.js → AI plane → NIM, plus per-call token / cost / latency metrics, dashboards, and alerts.
  • Why. Logs tell you what happened, not what it cost or where the time went across three hops.
  • Unlocks. Cost you can attribute per capability, latency you can trace to a plane, and alerts before users notice.
Scale-out

Shared rate-limit store

  • What. Replace the in-memory token-bucket limiter with a shared store (Redis).
  • Why. The current limiter is per-instance, so a second app or AI-plane replica would each keep their own budget and blow past the tier limit.
  • Unlocks. Horizontal scaling of the app and AI plane without breaking the model request budget.
Deployment

Ship the Azure design

  • What. The Azure reference architecture is a design; implement it as Bicep IaC plus GitHub Actions (OIDC), with the private, managed-identity posture described.
  • Why. A drawing isn’t a deployment. Repeatable infra is what makes an in-trust install real.
  • Unlocks. A one-command, reviewable path into a hospital tenant, keeping clinical data inside it.
Foundation

Re-introduce CI

  • What. CI is currently deferred (gates run locally). Bring back GitHub Actions — lint · typecheck · test · build · eval gates.
  • Why. Several roadmap items — the eval gates, the Azure pipeline — depend on a CI backbone.
  • Unlocks. Every quality gate enforced automatically, on every change.

Consolidate the About-guide source

Docs hygiene

What. public/about-architecture.html and public/about-azure.html are hand-maintained — only about.html mirrors docs/guide.html via pnpm sync:about. Consolidate their source to a single origin.

  • Why it matters. Two hand-kept copies drift; a reader can’t tell which page is current, and the guide is how the product explains itself.
  • What it unlocks. One source of truth for the in-app guide, so every About page stays honest without manual sync.

Today only about.html is generated; the architecture and Azure pages are maintained by hand.

The through-line. Everything above follows one rule that also governs what shipped: WardBeat earns each new capability rather than assuming it. It recommends before it acts, measures before it claims, and stays inside the trust before it touches real data. The gaps on this page aren’t missing features — they’re the next capabilities to earn, each behind a human gate, a measured number, or a governance review. Decision-support first; autonomy only where it’s proven safe.
