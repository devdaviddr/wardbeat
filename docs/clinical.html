The problem

A capacity problem that's really an information problem

Wards lose bed capacity not because patients can't leave, but because patient flow stalls and the reason is hard to see. The signal that matters lives in prose, and coordination is manual.

Invisible discharge barriersA patient is medically fit for discharge but stuck on TTOs, transport, social care, or a review — and no one has one current view of why.

Signal buried in notesDischarge status, EDD, and barriers live in free-text nursing and ward-round notes, not structured fields.

Reactive, not plannedTeams firefight in the afternoon; "discharge before noon" targets slip, and delayed transfers of care (DTOC) build up.

High-friction coordinationBoard rounds and handovers are assembled by hand from scattered systems, eating clinical time.

Who uses it

The people running flow

Bed / flow manager

"Free the right beds by noon, safely." Lives on the ward board and the action queue.

Charge nurse

"Know each patient's barrier without re-reading every note." Uses the per-bed barrier cards + citations.

Discharge coordinator

"Chase TTOs, transport, and social care before they block a bed." Works the recommended actions.

Site / operations manager

"See house-wide flow risk for the next 12–24h." Reads the net-position flow briefing.

Clinical governance lead

"Trust that the AI is grounded, audited, and safe." Relies on citations + the action audit trail.

A morning on the ward

How it fits the working day

Ward board

08:00 — open the board

The whole ward at a glance: every bed, who's in it, who's medically fit, and an amber chip on the beds with an open barrier. The fit-but-delayed filter shows exactly which beds could free today.

Run extraction

Read the overnight notes

One click extracts barriers/EDD from the latest notes. Each barrier links to the exact sentence it came from, so the charge nurse doesn't re-read every entry.

Actions panel

Turn barriers into a worklist

Generate recommended next-best actions — "chase TTOs with pharmacy", "book transport" — each justified by trust discharge policy. The coordinator approves the ones to chase; every approval is audited. Nothing acts on its own.

Copilot

Ask the ward a question

"Which patients are fit but waiting on transport?" — the answer highlights those beds. "What are the criteria for discharging on IV antibiotics?" — a grounded answer citing the policy.

Flow briefing

Brief the site meeting

A generated summary: net bed position for the next 12h, likely discharges, and the beds to chase — the numbers from a forecast model, the words from the AI. Ready for the 10:00 flow huddle.

Today's scope is decision-support. WardBeat surfaces, explains, and recommends. Actually recording a discharge (and freeing the bed) is the next step on the roadmap — right now the loop is "see and decide", not "act on the record".

What it changes

The outcomes it targets

Time to spot MFFD-but-delayed patients — from minutes of reading to a glance.
Discharge-before-noon rate — plan the morning instead of firefighting the afternoon.
Board-round / handover prep — an auto-generated briefing instead of manual assembly.
Bed utilisation vs the safe ceiling — fewer beds stuck on a chasable barrier.

Safety & governance

Built for a clinical setting

Decision-support, not a device

Non-diagnostic; it recommends and drafts, clinicians decide. Documented intended use, out of scope for regulated medical-device function.

Human-in-the-loop

No autonomous clinical action. Every recommended action is a proposal a person approves, and each decision is attributable in an audit trail.

Grounded & cited

Every barrier and answer cites its source; unsupported output is dropped or refused, not shown. Evidence over assertion.

Data stays in the trust

Runs entirely on synthetic data today (zero PHI). The AI models are self-hostable, so patient-adjacent data can stay on the hospital's own infrastructure — the deciding factor for clinical data.

What it is not

Deliberate non-goals

Not an EHR or bed-management system of record — a thin layer on top. Not an autonomous agent. Not a diagnostic or triage tool. In this version, not connected to real patient data or live pharmacy/transport systems — every patient and note here is synthetic.
