WardBeat

WardBeat reads a hospital ward’s notes and shows who is medically fit to leave and what is blocking them, so beds free up sooner and more safely.

● live on NVIDIA NIM Next.js 16 · FastAPI · Postgres/pgvector
01

The problem

Wards lose bed capacity not because patients cannot leave, but because flow stalls and the reason is hard to see. The status that matters — who is medically fit, and what is blocking their discharge — lives in free-text notes, not structured fields. Finding it means re-reading notes and chasing people by hand.

Signal buried in notes

Discharge status, the expected date, and every barrier sit in free-text nursing and ward-round notes.

Invisible barriers

A patient is fit to go but stuck on medications, transport, social care, or a review, with no single view of why.

Reactive coordination

Teams firefight in the afternoon. Board rounds are assembled by hand, and discharges slip past noon.

02

What WardBeat does

WardBeat reads every note on the ward and turns it into a live board: who is fit to leave, what is blocking each patient, and what to do next. Every answer is grounded in a real note or policy passage you can click through to.

Ward board

Every bed at a glance, with its barriers and discharge probability. The single view the ward was missing.

Copilot

Ask about the live ward or discharge policy in plain language. Answers cite their source, or say they do not know.

Actions

For each barrier, a suggested next step grounded in policy. A person approves it; nothing acts on its own.

Flow briefing

A short shift summary and the net bed position, with the numbers from a model and the words from the AI.

Decision-support, not autonomy. WardBeat recommends and drafts; clinicians decide. Every AI output is grounded and cited, no model takes a clinical action on its own, and the whole system runs on synthetic data with zero patient information.
03

Architecture

Four planes behind a single front door. Next.js is the only thing the browser talks to, and the only thing that writes to the database. The AI work runs in a small internal service that holds no state, so it is easy to scale, test, and move on-prem.

Front door

Next.js 16

  • The only surface the browser reaches
  • Orchestrates the AI work and is the sole database writer
AI plane

FastAPI

  • Extraction, retrieval, the action agent, forecasting
  • Stateless and internal; it never touches the database
Models

NVIDIA NIM

  • Nemotron, embedding, and rerank models
  • Hosted for the demo; on-prem in production
Data

Postgres + pgvector

  • Ward data, AI output, and the policy vectors
  • One database, no separate vector store

One request, end to end

The AI plane is a toolbox of model calls, and Next.js drives them. A retrieval question, for example, is Next.js embedding the query, running the vector search itself (that is where the database lives), then asking the AI plane to rerank the results and write a cited answer. Deployment

The whole stack runs from Docker. The hosted demo and an on-prem install use the same containers and the same OpenAI-compatible model contract, so moving into a hospital is a config change — swap the model URL and key — not a rewrite. That is what lets clinical data stay inside the trust.

04

Stack

TypeScript runs the product and Python runs the AI, because the AI ecosystem lives in Python. One internal service, one front door, one database.

Next.js 16 · React 19

App Router, Server Components, and Server Actions. The UI, auth, and the API-for-the-frontend in one framework.

FastAPI · Python 3.12

A small, stateless AI service: extraction, retrieval, the action agent, and forecasting.

NVIDIA NIM

Self-hostable models behind an OpenAI-compatible API, so data can stay on the trust’s own hardware.

PostgreSQL 17 · pgvector

One database for the ward data and the policy vectors, with type-safe migrations via Drizzle.
